1. Overview, Target Users, and Value



WorkPulse is a web-based employee attendance and work analytics information system for MVL. The researcher’s interview clarification identifies manual payroll preparation and possible time-in records for employees who are not physically present as the main concerns. The system combines attendance, employee records, leave, cash advances, payroll summaries, notifications, and reporting.

| Item | WorkPulse approach |

| --- | --- |

| Target users | MVL employees: Regular, Extra, and Manager / Supervisor classifications |

| Administrative stakeholders | MVL owners and authorized administrators |

| Potential paying party | MVL management; employees have no individual access fee |

| Business model | One-time system implementation payment, with maintenance paid separately when requested |

| Current deployment | Free-tier Vercel frontend and Render backend, as reported by the developer |



The workplace is in Salay Riles, Mangaldan, Pangasinan. Chapter 2 identifies 30 study participants: two owners, two managers/supervisors, ten regular employees, and sixteen extra employees. This is a study population, not a count of interviews or paying customers.

Value Proposition



| MVL problem | WorkPulse response | Expected benefit |

| --- | --- | --- |

| Manual payroll preparation | Prepare payroll using recorded attendance, configured rates, deductions, and carried earnings; show payslip summaries | Reduce repeated calculation and support review of employee earnings |

| Possible “ghost attendance”: recorded time-in without physical presence | Fingerprint time-in/time-out at the local workstation; attendance source indicators and correction history | Support identity checks and review of proxy-attendance concerns |

| Attendance and work-hour discrepancies | Recorded sessions, date-range views, and validated administrator corrections | Help reconcile records before payroll settlement |

| Limited employee visibility | Personal attendance and payroll portal, request status, and notifications | Help employees understand records affecting their pay |



These needs are qualitative findings from Chapters 1–3 and the interview clarification. “Ghost attendance” describes a concern, not proven fraud. A fingerprint scan verifies an attendance event, not continuous presence throughout a shift. Payroll accuracy depends on correct inputs and authorized review; benefits must be measured.

2. Pricing, Payment, and Maintenance



The team proposes a ₱20,000–₱50,000 one-time implementation package for MVL, depending on the agreement with MVL and the setup and delivery scope. This is the team’s proposed valuation, not an agreed purchase or verified market price. Employee portal access is included; there is no monthly software subscription.

| Charge | Proposed price | Scope |

| --- | --- | --- |

| System implementation | ₱20,000–₱50,000 once | Agreed existing features, configuration, deployment, record setup, installation, testing, training, and handover |

| Routine maintenance | ₱500–₱2,000 per job | Depends on the issue, effort, and complexity; examples include minor fixes, troubleshooting, and routine updates |

| Major maintenance | Separate quote | Complex recovery, migration, extensive repairs, or work beyond routine maintenance; may exceed ₱2,000 |

| New features | Separate quote | Additional modules or substantial feature changes; priced by scope and effort |

| Fingerprint equipment | Separate quote if needed | Reader, workstation, and hardware not included in the system price |

| Current hosting | ₱0 assumed provider charge | Reported free-tier Vercel and Render deployment; future paid infrastructure budgeted separately |



Payment Options



MVL may pay in full or through agreed milestones. Example only: a ₱25,000 package could be paid as ₱10,000 upon agreement, ₱10,000 after deployment and user testing, and ₱5,000 at final acceptance. Installments change timing, not the total price; the final schedule requires MVL’s agreement.

Maintenance Process



MVL reports an issue or requests a change. The team provides a job-specific quote, obtains MVL’s agreement, completes and verifies the work, and invoices as agreed. Routine jobs are proposed at ₱500–₱2,000; major work and new features require separate quotes. No automatic monthly charge or annual renewal is proposed.

| Service term | Proposed agreement |

| --- | --- |

| Included corrections | Specify an agreed post-delivery correction period and covered defects; do not charge separately for work already included |

| Acceptance and handover | Define acceptance checks, documentation, accounts, usage rights, and any source-code transfer |

| Operational responsibilities | Assign deployment, backups, monitoring, and record-export responsibilities; continuous support requires a separate arrangement |

| Future hosting upgrades | Confirm provider suitability and actual charges, agree the budget, and identify who pays before upgrading |



Free hosting does not make maintenance labor free. Wages, cash advances, repayments, and carried payroll balances remain employer/employee funds and are not WorkPulse revenue. WorkPulse records settlement and disbursement confirmation; it does not itself transfer funds through a bank or payment gateway.

3. Cost Computation, Income, and Break-even



The proposed system valuation is ₱20,000–₱50,000. Check actual effort and expenses before choosing a final amount. The routine maintenance range is ₱500–₱2,000 per requested job; major work and new features are priced separately. Charges to the client must not be added again as expenses.

| Cost item | Planning computation |

| --- | --- |

| Implementation labor | Estimated hours × agreed internal hourly cost, including configuration, installation, testing, training, and handover |

| Maintenance labor | Hours for the requested job × internal hourly cost |

| Travel, materials, and equipment | Actual job-related expenses not already counted in labor or supplied by MVL |

| Vercel and Render hosting | Current reported free-tier subscription expense: ₱0; paid upgrades costed separately if needed |

| Other infrastructure | Confirm database, backups, connectivity, and hardware costs; free frontend/backend hosting does not establish that all infrastructure is free |



Revenue Planning for One MVL Deployment



| Scenario | Illustrative gross income |

| --- | --- |

| System package only | ₱20,000–₱50,000 once, depending on agreed price |

| Example: ₱25,000 package + two ₱1,000 routine jobs | ₱27,000 total; job count and amounts are assumptions |

| Following years | Only commissioned maintenance and new-feature income; no repeat system fee |

| No requested paid work | ₱0 maintenance income for that period |



Total service income = implementation fee + maintenance job fees + additional-feature fees. Record the implementation payment once. Track invoices and collections separately. Future maintenance orders and renewals are not guaranteed.

Break-even Logic



Implementation cost = labor + direct delivery expenses + any allocated overhead. The implementation fee covers delivery costs when it is at least this total. For maintenance, the job fee must cover that job’s labor, direct expenses, and any allocated overhead. Count every cost once.

Estimated remaining balance = service income − all counted costs. For a ₱25,000 package, cost coverage occurs when all counted implementation costs are at most ₱25,000. A ₱1,000 maintenance job covers its costs only if those costs are at most ₱1,000. Actual profit requires actual expenses, taxes, and collections; no costs or profit are invented here.

Benefits to MVL and the Development Team



MVL employees gain visibility into their attendance and pay records; administrators gain a more consistent payroll and correction workflow. The development team may earn implementation and requested-maintenance income and gain deployment experience. Time savings, fewer discrepancies, and financial returns depend on actual use, review results, costs, and payment collection.

4. Pilot Plan, Success Measures, and Risks



Run a proposed 30-day MVL pilot to check payroll preparation, attendance traceability, and employee record access. Keep independently reviewed source records for comparison. This is a validation plan, not an achieved result.

| Stage | Timing | Activities |

| --- | --- | --- |

| Baseline and setup | Days 1–7 | Record manual payroll preparation time; confirm rates, accounts, and attendance procedures; test the local scanner and train users |

| Guided operation | Days 8–21 | Exercise attendance and employee portal workflows; compare a payroll cycle or controlled payroll examples with independently checked records |

| Review and acceptance | Days 22–30 | Review discrepancies, time spent, user feedback, access controls, and recovery; agree delivery acceptance and any further work |



Proposed Success Measures



| Measure | Target or review method |

| --- | --- |

| Payroll preparation time | Aim for 20% less time than a comparable manual baseline; measure rather than assume savings |

| Payroll reconciliation | Review all pilot payroll statements against approved attendance, rates, and deductions |

| Attendance traceability | Check source indicators and required reasons/history for manual entries and corrections |

| Employee access | Confirm participating employees can view their own attendance and payroll summaries and raise discrepancies |

| Presence concerns | Log reported concerns and verified outcomes separately; do not assume a zero-fraud result |



Key Risks and Mitigation



| Risk | Mitigation |

| --- | --- |

| Incorrect pay or false-presence records | Validate inputs; require payroll review; inspect attendance sources and correction history |

| Scanner, network, or hosting interruption | Test local components and recovery; use approved, documented manual contingencies |

| Sensitive employee data exposure or loss | Validate ownership controls, restricted administration, logs, backups, and restoration |

| Unclear maintenance or funding | Define included corrections and ongoing responsibilities; agree job quotes before paid work |
