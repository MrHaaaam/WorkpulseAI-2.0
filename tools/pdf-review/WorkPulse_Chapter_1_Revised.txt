Chapter 1
INTRODUCTION
Background of the Study
Employee  attendance  and  work  information  management  is  a  vital  component  of 
organizational  operations,  as  it  directly  affects  productivity,  accountability,  and  
decisionmaking.  Globally,  organizations  are  increasingly  adopting  digital  and  analytics-
driven human resource systems to replace manual and outdated attendance methods that  
often  result  in  errors,  inefficiencies,  and  limited  access  to  actionable  insights.  Recent 
literature highlights that the integration of biometric-based system is revolutionizing human  
resource  management  by  enhancing  operational  efficiency,  minimizing  labor-related  
inefficiencies, and eliminating the administrative burdens of traditional tracking methods  
(ljiga  et  al.,  2025).  Modern  workforce  analytics  systems  enable  organizations  to  record 
attendance accurately, monitor work patterns, and generate data-driven reports that support  
effective human resource management. Despite these global advancements, studies reveal  
that many organizations still rely on disconnected or partly manual processes to manage  
employee  information,  attendance,  leave,  and  payroll.  These  practices  often  result  in 
inconsistent records, delayed approvals, limited employee transparency, inefficient reporting,  
and  weak  accountability.  Basic  systems  may  also  lack  appropriate  authentication,  
authorization, audit logging, and protection for sensitive employee information, which can  
compromise employee trust and organizational credibility. Furthermore, traditional payroll  
and security systems such as manual logs or PIN codes contribute heavily to human error and 
unauthorized  entry,  explicitly  highlighting  the  critical  need  for  biometric  verification  to 
eliminate proxy attendance practices like "buddy punching" (International Journal of Science  
and  Research  Archive,  2026).  In  the  Philippine  context,  organizations  are  rapidly 
transitioning toward digital transformation in response to the growing demand for efficient,  
transparent,  and  technology-driven  workplace  systems.  The  Philippine  HR  technology 
market is experiencing significant growth as employers increasingly rely on cloud-based  
platforms and digital portals for faster, more efficient workforce processing (IMARC Group,  
2025). This push for digitalization is evident even at the national level, with recent milestone  
initiatives like the Philippine Civil Service Modernization Project launched in late 2025,  
which seeks to harness digital tools to automate human resource and payroll systems for

better efficiency and transparency (Civil Service Commission, 2025). However, many private 
and local organizations continue to face challenges such as fragmented attendance records,  
inefficient monitoring of work hours, and limited analytical capability. A centralized and  
secure workforce information management system is therefore needed to improve record  
management,  administrative  workflows,  employee  access,  and  data-supported  decision-
making. In response to these challenges, this study proposes WorkPulse, a secure web-based  
workforce  information  management  system  with  integrated  fingerprint-based  attendance 
verification. The project aims to centralize employee, attendance, leave, and payroll records  
while providing strict role-based access organized into administrator and employee access  
groups. By utilizing a fingerprint scanner to verify employee identity during time-in and  
timeout,  the  system  aims  to  associate  biometric  attendance  records  with  the  verified 
employee, improving identity assurance and operational integrity. Data is securely managed  
using  a  cloud-hosted  MongoDB  Atlas  platform  to  support  centralized,  authorized  data 
storage and access. This study aligns with Sustainable Development Goal (SDG) 8: Decent  
Work  and  Economic  Growth  by  promoting  fair  labor  practices,  and  SDG  9:  Industry, 
Innovation, and Infrastructure by integrating digital analytics and secure hardware solutions  
into organizational process.
Statement of the Problem
Many  organizations  manage  employee  information,  attendance,  leave,  and  payroll 
through disconnected or partly manual processes. These practices often lead to inaccurate  
records, lack of transparency, inefficient workforce planning, and weak security controls that  
may expose sensitive employee data. In view of the foregoing, this study seeks to answer the  
following questions: 1. What challenges are encountered in managing employee information,  
attendance, leave, and payroll through current manual or fragmented processes? 2. What  
functional  and  security  requirements  should  WorkPulse  provide  for  administrators  and 
employees? 3. How can the system centralize workforce records and support attendance  
monitoring,  leave  processing,  payroll  workflows,  and  employee  self-service?  4.  How 
acceptable is the developed system in terms of functional suitability, usability, performance  
efficiency,  reliability,  and  security?  5.  How  accurately  and  reliably  does  the  integrated 
fingerprint verification authenticate employee attendance?

Objectives of the Study
The primary objective of this study is to design, develop, and evaluate WorkPulse, a  
secure  web-based  workforce  information  management  system  that  centralizes  employee 
records, attendance, leave, cash advance requests, and payroll workflows using MongoDB  
Atlas;  provides  administrator  and  employee  access  groups;  supports  fingerprint  and 
authorized manual attendance processes; and offers workforce reporting and implemented  
analytical features.
The specific objectives of this study are:
1. To identify problems and requirements in existing employee information, attendance,  
leave, cash advance, and payroll processes.
2. To develop a centralized web-based system integrated with MongoDB Atlas for employee  
records, attendance monitoring, leave management, cash advance processing, and payroll  
workflows.
3. To implement administrator and employee access groups with authenticated access, record  
ownership controls, and support for Regular, Extra, and Manager / Supervisor employee  
classifications.
4. To provide an employee portal for viewing authorized personal, attendance, payroll, and  
leave  information; submitting  leave  and  cash advance  requests; and  receiving  employee 
notifications.
5.  To  implement  appropriate  security  controls,  including  authentication,  authorization, 
session  management,  request  protection,  input  validation,  audit  logging,  and  controlled 
administrator attendance corrections with required reasons, correction history, and protection  
of attendance already included in settled payroll.
6. To generate descriptive dashboards and reports from stored workforce records and provide  
only those analytical forecasting functions that are implemented and evaluated; to support  
limited offline saving and later synchronization for designated activities.
7.  To  evaluate  the  system  using  defined  software-quality  criteria  and  test  procedures, 
including  the  operation  of  employee  notifications,  cash  advance  workflows,  manual 
attendance corrections, and offline synchronization where implemented.

Scope and Delimitation
This study covers the design and development of WorkPulse as a responsive, web-based 
workforce information management system using a cloud-hosted MongoDB Atlas database.  
The system has two access groups: administrator and employee. Employee classifications  
consist  of  Regular,  Extra,  and  Manager  /  Supervisor.  These  classifications  describe 
employees and do not establish additional system access groups or a separate management  
portal.
The  attendance  component  includes  time-in  and  time-out  through  a  configured 
fingerprint attendance kiosk, as well as authorized administrator entry of manual attendance  
and corrections. Administrators must provide a reason for each correction, and the system  
retains a correction history for accountability. Attendance records already included in settled  
payroll  are  protected  from  direct  editing;  any  necessary  adjustment  must  follow  an 
authorized, traceable procedure rather than silently changing settled records.
The  employee  portal supports viewing authorized personal information, attendance,  
leave, payroll and payslip summaries, submitting leave requests and cash advance requests,  
and receiving employee notifications about relevant actions or status changes. Cash advance  
workflows  cover  request  submission,  administrative  review,  disbursement  confirmation, 
outstanding  balance  tracking,  repayment  deductions,  carried  balances  between  payroll 
periods, and related payslip summaries. Payroll processing is subject to authorized review  
and settlement controls.
The system includes authentication and email OTP where configured, role-based access  
control, employee information management, system settings, audit logging, and descriptive  
workforce  dashboards.  Descriptive  reporting  summarizes  recorded  events  and  totals; 
analytical  forecasting  is  a  distinct  function.  The  forecasting  scope  is  limited  to  the 
implemented and evaluated functions: estimates of expected employee attendance over the  
next seven days, estimates of attendance-risk events over the next 30 days, and identification  
of unusual arrival patterns. Forecasting outputs are not equivalent to measured employee  
productivity, and  any statistical  fallback  must be  distinguished  from a  trained machine-
learning result.

The system provides limited offline saving for designated manual attendance entries or  
corrections,  leave  requests,  cash  advance  requests,  and  viewing  information  previously 
cached on the device. Eligible offline actions are held in an offline queue and synchronized  
when  connectivity  returns,  subject  to  server-side  validation,  authorization,  and  conflict 
handling.
Fingerprint-based attendance requires the supported fingerprint scanner connected to  
the configured local attendance workstation, together with its reader and matching software.  
Fingerprint capture and verification cannot be performed solely through a remote cloud  
browser session. The system does not include facial recognition or webcam-based attendance  
verification.
Cloud access to WorkPulse is separate from the local fingerprint workstation: web  
access to the deployed application and synchronized MongoDB Atlas records depends on the  
configured server, network availability, authentication services, and browser compatibility.  
Limited offline support does not make the entire system fully operational without internet  
connectivity. Fingerprint scanning, payments or disbursement execution, approval decisions,  
and  fresh  login  require  connectivity.  Offline  entries  remain  pending  until  successful 
synchronization and server-side checks.
WorkPulse supports workforce reporting and only the forecasting functions actually  
implemented  and  evaluated;  it  does  not  provide  a  complete  measurement  of  individual 
employee productivity. Cash advance tracking and payroll summaries do not themselves  
constitute  integration  with  a  banking  or  payment  gateway.  The  study  does  not  claim 
biometric  accuracy,  forecasting  performance,  or  offline  reliability  beyond  the  results 
demonstrated in the corresponding testing chapters.
Significance of the Study
This study aims to contribute to improved workforce management and organizational  
efficiency.  The  system  may  benefit  the  following:  Administrators  and  Management. 
Administrators can efficiently track verifiable employee attendance, manage leave approvals,  
process payroll workflows, and make informed decisions using centralized data dashboards  
hosted on a scalable cloud database. The system helps centralize workforce records, reduce  
repetitive administrative work, and provides a highly reliable method of confirming physical  
attendance  through  biometric  integration.  Employees.  Employees  benefit  from  accurate

Definition of Terms
The following terms are operationally defined as used in this study:
Attendance Correction.  An authorized change to an attendance record, including a  
manual adjustment, for which an administrator records a reason and the system retains a  
traceable  correction  history.  Records  included  in  settled  payroll  cannot  be  directly 
overwritten.
Attendance  Monitoring.  The  viewing,  tracking,  and  management  of  employee 
attendance records, including time-in, time-out, authorized manual entries, and corrections.
Audit  Logging  (Security  Audit  Logging).  The  recording  of  important  user, 
administrative,  and  security  events,  including  attendance  corrections,  to  support  
accountability and review.
Authentication. The process of verifying a user’s identity before permitting access to  
WorkPulse.
Authorization. The determination of which features and records an authenticated user  
may access based on their access group and record ownership.
Biometric Verification. The use of a fingerprint captured by the configured scanner to  
verify an employee’s identity for supported attendance transactions.
Carried Balance. The remaining unpaid amount of a cash advance that is transferred to  
a subsequent payroll period after applicable repayments are recorded.
Cash Advance. An employee request for an advance amount, subject to administrative  
review, confirmed disbursement, balance tracking, and repayment recording.

Cloud Database  (Cloud-Based Data  Storage).  The  remote  database  infrastructure 
used to store authorized WorkPulse workforce records; the project uses MongoDB Atlas.
Dashboard.  An  interface  presenting  summarized  workforce  information  through 
indicators, tables, charts, and reports.
Data  Validation.  Checking  entered  or  synchronized  information  against  required 
formats and business rules to reduce invalid records.
Descriptive Analytics.  The summarization of historical or current workforce records  
using counts, tables, charts, and reports without forecasting future outcomes.
Employee. An authorized user in the employee access group who may be classified as  
Regular, Extra, or Manager / Supervisor and may access permitted self-service features.
Employee Information Management.  The centralized organization and maintenance  
of employee profiles and related workforce records by authorized users.
Employee  Notification.  A  system-generated  notice  informing  an  employee  about 
relevant record updates, request decisions, or other actions available in WorkPulse.
Employee Portal (Employee Self-Service Portal). The employee-facing interface for  
viewing authorized personal, attendance, leave, payroll, and payslip information, submitting  
requests, and receiving notifications.
Encryption.  A security technique that transforms information to help protect it from  
unauthorized access.
Fingerprint Scanner. The supported local biometric hardware device used to capture  
fingerprint data for verification.
Fingerprint  Verification.  The  process of  comparing a  captured  fingerprint against 
authorized reference data to verify an employee during biometric attendance.
Fingerprint-Based Attendance.  An attendance transaction that uses the configured  
fingerprint reader and matching software to verify identity before recording time-in or time-
out.
Leave Management. The submission, review, approval or rejection, and recording of  
employee leave requests.

Manager  /  Supervisor.  An  employee  classification  within  WorkPulse,  alongside 
Regular and Extra; it does not automatically create a separate system access group.
MongoDB  Atlas.  The  cloud-hosted  database  platform  used  to  manage  WorkPulse 
workforce records.
Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA). An authentication approach requiring more than  
one verification factor; WorkPulse incorporates email OTP as an additional access measure  
where configured.
Offline Queue. Temporary local storage of eligible actions created without connectivity 
and awaiting validation and synchronization with the server.
One-Time Password (OTP). A temporary code sent to an authorized email address for  
an applicable authentication step.
Payroll  Management  (Payroll  Workflow).  The  authorized  recording,  review, 
calculation-related processing, and settlement of payroll information, including supported  
cash advance repayments and carried balances.
Payroll Summary / Payslip View. The employee-accessible display of authorized pay  
information, including relevant cash advance deductions and remaining balances.
Performance Efficiency.  The ability of the system to respond to user actions and  
process supported tasks without unnecessary delays.
Predictive Insights (Analytical Forecasting). Outputs of implemented and evaluated  
workforce analytical functions that estimate future attendance or attendance-risk events or  
flag unusual arrival patterns. They are separate from descriptive reports and are not a direct  
measurement of productivity.
Role-Based Access Control (RBAC). A security control that limits access according to 
the administrator or employee access group and applicable record permissions; Regular,  
Extra, and Manager / Supervisor are employee classifications.
Secure Authentication. Verification of user identity through configured controls such  
as credentials and email OTP.
Session  Management.  The  creation,  validation,  maintenance,  and  termination  of 
authenticated user sessions.

Synchronization. The transfer and reconciliation of eligible offline queued actions with 
the server once connectivity returns, subject to authorization and validation.
System Enhancement. The improvement or addition of system functions, controls, or  
other capabilities.
System Evaluation. The assessment of WorkPulse against defined usability, functional, 
security, performance, and reliability criteria.
System Quality. The quality of WorkPulse as determined through the stated evaluation  
criteria and observed results.
System Usability Scale (SUS).  A ten-item, five-point questionnaire  used to assess  
perceived system usability.
Time-In and Time-Out. The recording of the beginning and ending of an employee’s  
work period through fingerprint verification or authorized administrator manual attendance,  
as applicable.
Web-Based  Workforce  Information  Management  System.  A  browser-accessible 
system for managing employee, attendance, leave, cash advance, payroll, reporting, and  
security functions.
Weighted  Mean.  The  computed  average  rating  used  to  summarize  responses  to  a 
system evaluation criterion.
WorkPulse.  The  web-based  employee  attendance  and  work  analytics  information 
system developed for MVL, including the workforce modules and controls defined in this  
study.
Workforce Analytics Dashboard. A presentation of descriptive workforce indicators  
and reports, distinct from separately identified forecasting functions.
Workforce Information Management. The centralized organization and processing of 
employee-related records and workforce operations.
Workforce Planning.  The use of workforce information and evaluated analytics to  
inform organizational staffing and operational decisions.
