Chapter 2
METHODOLOGY
This chapter presents the methodology used in the design, development, testing, and evaluation 
of WorkPulse: Employee Attendance and Work Analytics Information System for MVL. It 
describes the development model, Scrum activities, deployed system architecture, tools and 
technologies, respondent population, data gathering, treatment of data, biometric evaluation 
procedures, data security, and ethical considerations.
WorkPulse centralizes employee, attendance, leave, payroll, cash advance, account, settings, 
biometric, and audit records. The system provides administrator and employee access groups; 
employee classifications include Regular, Extra, and Manager / Supervisor. Descriptive reports 
summarize recorded data, while predictive functions, where implemented and evaluated, 
provide separate analytical insights for administrative review.
Development Methodology
Software Development Model. The study adopts the Agile Software Development Model using 
Scrum. Requirements, reported defects, and recommended improvements are prioritized in a 
product backlog and addressed through iterative sprints. At the start of each sprint, the 
researchers identify deliverables and acceptance criteria; implemented functions are reviewed 
and tested, and unfinished tasks return to the backlog.

Scrum Development Phases
Planning. The researchers identify existing employee-information, attendance, leave, cash 
advance, payroll, and reporting problems and translate them into functional, security, usability, 
and deployment requirements. Planning includes fingerprint attendance, administrator manual 
attendance and corrections, correction history, employee notifications, limited offline saving, 
payroll settlement protections, authentication, role-based access, and audit logging. Daily and 
date-range attendance filtering are included in the final-version verification checklist; their 
completion must be confirmed against the deployed application before being reported as tested.

Design. The researchers design the database collections, user interfaces, authorization 
boundaries, fingerprint enrollment and attendance procedures, leave and cash advance requests, 
payroll workflows, offline queue, synchronization process, and analytical reporting. The 
employee-management module maintains employee profiles, classifications, assignments, and 
other authorized employment records. The payroll module manages authorized pay inputs, 
payroll computation and review, cash advance deductions, carried balances, settlement, and 
employee payslip summaries. Payroll settlement prevents unauthorized retroactive attendance 
changes affecting finalized payroll.
Development. The React frontend implements the administrator workspace, employee portal, 
and attendance kiosk. The Node.js and Express.js backend implements protected APIs, 
business rules, validation, attendance processing, leave and cash advance workflows, payroll 
processing, analytics, notifications, and audit logging. MongoDB Atlas stores authorized cloud 
records. IndexedDB and service worker caching support specifically designated offline actions 
and previously cached content.
Testing. Functional tests cover login, account ownership, employee management, fingerprint 
enrollment and attendance, manual attendance and corrections, leave, cash advances, payroll, 
reports, notifications, offline queue, and synchronization. Boundary and negative tests cover 
future-dated attendance times, overlapping attendance sessions, attempts to edit attendance 
already included in settled payroll, unauthorized access, stale cached records, duplicate 
synchronization submissions, network interruptions, and local-storage failures. For each test, 
the researchers record the input, expected result, actual result, and disposition; the manuscript 
must not imply a test passed unless evidence exists.
Deployment. The frontend is hosted on Vercel and accessed through a web browser. The 
frontend sends authenticated HTTPS requests to the Node.js/Express.js backend hosted on 
Render. The Render backend enforces server-side authorization and business rules and reads or 
writes cloud records in MongoDB Atlas. A configured local fingerprint workstation hosts the 
compatible scanner, reader interface, and any locally required template extraction or matching 
components. Scanner access is local to that workstation; a remote Render service cannot 
directly read USB scanner hardware or execute software installed only on the workstation.
Limited Offline Operation. IndexedDB stores permitted queued actions and selected cached 
records in the browser; service worker caching supports designated application resources.

Subject to final implementation verification, offline users may queue authorized manual 
attendance/corrections, leave requests, and cash advance requests and view previously cached 
information. Cached records may be outdated. When connectivity returns, queued items are 
synchronized to the server, revalidated against current permissions and rules, and marked 
Synced or Needs Review. Fingerprint scanning, payments, approvals, fresh login, and 
operations requiring live server verification are not available offline. WorkPulse is not a fully 
offline system.
System Architecture Overview
WorkPulse uses a multi-layered architecture connecting browser-based presentation, server-
side application services, cloud data storage, and locally operated fingerprint components. 
Fingerprint capture and verification replace the unrelated facial-recognition description in the 
earlier manuscript.
Presentation Tier. React 19, TypeScript, Vite, Tailwind CSS, and Recharts provide 
administrator, employee, and kiosk interfaces. IndexedDB supports browser-local queued 
records, and a service worker caches eligible application assets. User interface visibility is not a 
substitute for backend authorization.
Application Tier. Node.js and Express.js on Render provide authenticated REST endpoints, 
enforce role-based access and record ownership, validate requests, prevent duplicate 
synchronization, protect settled payroll, and handle payroll, attendance, leave, cash advance, 
notification, and audit workflows.
Data Tier. MongoDB Atlas stores authorized employee, account, attendance, leave, cash 
advance, payroll, settings, biometric-related, session, and audit records. Browser-local data is 
temporary and must be reconciled with authoritative server records.
Local Biometric Tier. The configured fingerprint workstation connects to the DigitalPersona 
reader through locally installed reader components and the applicable WebSDK or bridge. 
FingerJet template extraction or matching runs only where the required native runtime and files 
are actually installed and accessible. If FingerJet is installed only on the local workstation, the 
local workstation performs that step and communicates the resulting permitted data or decision 
securely to the backend. The exact deployed processing location and trust boundary must be 
checked against the working source code and workstation setup.

Tools and Technologies
React 19, TypeScript, Vite, Tailwind CSS, and Recharts. Provide the responsive web 
interfaces, typed frontend development, bundling, styling, and graphical reporting.
Node.js and Express.js. Provide the backend runtime, protected REST API routes, application 
services, and server-side validation. Node.js native scrypt with randomly generated salts is used 
for password derivation where implemented.
MongoDB Atlas and Mongoose/MongoDB operations. Provide cloud-hosted record storage 
and database access.
IndexedDB. Stores permitted browser-local cached records and pending offline submissions, 
subject to browser storage limitations and account separation.

Population and Locale of the Study
The study uses total enumeration of the 30 identified MVL respondents: two owners, two 
supervisors/managers, ten regular employees, and sixteen extra employees. These are 
organizational respondent categories rather than four separate WorkPulse login roles. The 
system uses administrator and employee access groups; Manager / Supervisor, Regular, and 
Extra are employee classifications. The two owners are counted as respondents and may 
perform administrator tasks according to their assigned access. The study does not add 
respondents when describing access groups.
The study locale is the MVL workplace in Salay Riles, Mangaldan, Pangasinan, Region I. 
Authorized actual records and suitable simulated records may be used during testing to limit 
exposure of personal information.

Data Gathering Techniques
Interviews. Semi-structured interviews with authorized MVL owners and supervisors/managers 
identify actual attendance, employee-information, leave, cash advance, payroll, policy, 
reporting, and operational requirements. A supervisor or manager participating as an employee 
uses the Manager / Supervisor classification rather than being described as a regular employee.
Survey Questionnaires. Eligible respondents answer the applicable evaluation questionnaire 
after performing authorized WorkPulse tasks. The questionnaire addresses selected software-
quality criteria such as functional suitability, usability, performance efficiency, reliability, and 
perceived security. Claims that three experts validated the instrument, a separate pilot test was 
conducted, or Cronbach’s alpha was computed require actual supporting documents and data. 
Without such evidence, these activities must be described as planned or omitted; no coefficient 
is asserted here.
Observation. Researchers document the existing MVL processes for attendance capture, 
attendance correction, employee records, leave, cash advances, payroll, and payslip 
distribution. Observations are used to identify bottlenecks and compare the implemented 
workflows with actual workplace practices.
Sources of Data
Primary data are obtained from the 2 owners, 2 supervisors/managers, 10 regular employees, 
and 16 extra employees who participate in interviews, observations, task-based testing, or 
questionnaires as applicable. Owners and supervisors/managers provide policy and approval 
requirements; regular and extra employees provide information on attendance and employee 
self-service experiences.
The researchers observe MVL workplace recordkeeping, attendance logging, leave processing, 
cash advance handling, payroll preparation, and report generation, subject to permission and 
privacy safeguards. These observations replace the unrelated school tracing and alumni

Biometric Evaluation Procedures
Participants and Consent. Invite authorized consenting participants who meet the study 
eligibility criteria. Record the actual number of enrolled participants and scan trials in the 
evaluation log; do not invent sample sizes. Explain the purpose of fingerprint testing, privacy 
measures, and the approved alternative attendance procedure.
Enrollment. At the configured workstation, enroll the designated fingerprint according to the 
reader and matching-software procedure. Record successful enrollments, capture failures, 
repeated attempts, and any exclusions. Separate failed captures from valid comparisons.
Genuine Identification Trials. An enrolled participant presents the finger enrolled in the 
evaluation database. The system searches enrolled templates. A correct identification is a 
genuine success; a failure to identify the enrolled participant is a false rejection when a valid 
comparison occurred.
Unrecognized / Impostor Trials. A person or finger not enrolled in the evaluation database is 
presented for one-to-many identification. The expected result is no match. Any match to an 
enrolled employee is a false acceptance. An enrolled participant presenting their enrolled finger 
is not an impostor trial.
Conditions and Timing. Document the scanner and software versions, workstation setup, 
connection status, finger condition where relevant, trial sequence, capture time, template 
processing time, matching time, and total response time. Report actual measurements and 
capture failures separately.
Outcome Calculation. For valid impostor comparisons, False Acceptance Rate (FAR) = false 
acceptances ÷ valid impostor comparisons × 100%. For valid genuine comparisons, False 
Rejection Rate (FRR) = false rejections ÷ valid genuine comparisons × 100%. Report the count 
of failed captures separately and state the denominator used. Actual values belong in the results 
chapter after testing.

Treatment of Data
Data Collection and Processing. Interview notes, survey responses, observation records, and 
testing logs are checked for completeness. Authorized datasets are organized consistently, with 
date and time formats standardized and duplicates identified without changing participants’ 
original responses.
Data Analysis. Descriptive summaries, frequencies, percentages, weighted means, and, where 
applicable, usability scores describe respondents’ evaluation and workforce records. 
Descriptive dashboards summarize historical information; predictive outputs are a separate 
function and should be reported only for models actually implemented and evaluated. Any 
trained model must be distinguished from statistical fallback outputs.
Data Storage and Backup. MongoDB Atlas stores authoritative cloud records subject to 
authentication, authorization, ownership restrictions, and server-side validation. IndexedDB 
temporarily stores permitted account-separated browser data and queued actions. Pending 
entries await submission, Synced entries have been accepted by the server, and Needs Review 
entries require user or administrator action after validation or conflict detection. 
Synchronization must recheck current access, attendance rules, payroll settlement, and record 
versions and prevent duplicate processing through unique operation identifiers or equivalent 
idempotency controls. Cached information may be stale, and browser storage can be cleared or 
fail. Backup and restoration claims must reflect the configured deployment and actual tests; 
cloud storage alone does not establish that a recovery test occurred.
Ethical Considerations
Security and Privacy of Data. WorkPulse handles employee records, attendance, leave, cash 
advances, payroll, and fingerprint biometric data/templates. Data processing should follow 
Republic Act No. 10173 and principles of transparency, legitimate purpose, proportionality, 
accountability, and reasonable safeguards. Only authorized processes should access biometric 
templates and sensitive workforce records.
Fair and Inclusive Design. Fingerprint attendance verifies identity; it is not a direct measure of 
employee performance. Employees who cannot complete fingerprint capture may use verified 
administrator manual attendance under an approved policy. The administrator records the 
reason, affected employee, time, and correction history, and the employee receives a 
notification when supported. Attendance included in settled payroll must not be silently edited;
