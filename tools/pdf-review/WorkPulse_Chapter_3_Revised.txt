CHAPTER 3
DISCUSSION OF FINDINGS
This chapter discusses the findings, identified workforce-management challenges, implemented 
WorkPulse modules, respondent evaluations, and the technical evidence required to substantiate 
system performance. Survey results describe respondents’ perceptions; functional, security, 
reliability, offline, and biometric claims require separately documented technical tests.

Implemented Features of WorkPulse
The following descriptions reflect the revised system scope. The final deployed version and its 
screenshots must be checked before each feature is described as verified by testing.
Employee Information Management
Authorized administrators manage centralized employee records. The employee classifications 
are Regular, Extra, and Manager / Supervisor. These classifications describe workforce categories 
and do not create separate application access groups. Any older screenshot showing Manager and 
Supervisor as separate options must be replaced with the verified combined classification.

Fingerprint-Based Attendance
The attendance kiosk supports fingerprint-based time-in and time-out through a configured local 
scanner workstation, reader drivers/WebSDK, and local fingerprint capture and matching 
components. The hosted Render backend does not directly access a USB scanner attached to a 
user’s computer. Fingerprint operation requires the supported workstation environment, scanner 
software, and connectivity to the relevant online attendance services. The exact tested operating 
system, device model, matching-software version, network conditions, and trial results must be 
recorded in the biometric evaluation subsection.
Figure 4. Fingerprint-Based Attendance (original manuscript image; replace if outdated).
Attendance Monitoring and Corrections
Administrators can view daily attendance and filter by date range. Authorized administrators can 
manually record attendance or correct eligible records, with a required reason, actor and 
timestamp history, and an employee notification. Attendance displays distinguish recorded and 
edited entries and identify their source. Attendance records included in settled payroll must not be 
silently edited; the system should reject prohibited changes and preserve the audit trail. These 
behaviors require final deployment verification.

Employee Self-Service Portal
Employees can view authorized personal records, attendance, leave, payroll and payslip 
information, and the status of cash advance requests. The notification bell surfaces relevant 
attendance corrections and other employee notices. Attendance entries indicate whether they were 
fingerprint-recorded or manually recorded/edited. Limited offline operation permits selected 
previously cached views and queues eligible actions, with clear warnings that cached information 
may be outdated. Authorized administrators, rather than a separate HR role, manage the relevant 
approvals and corrections.
Figure 6. Employee Self-Service Portal (original manuscript image; replace if outdated).
Leave Management
Employees submit leave requests and view their status. Authorized administrators review and act 
on requests. Eligible requests may be saved to a local offline queue and submitted after 
reconnection; an offline queue entry is not an approval.

Payroll Management and Payslip Summary
The payroll workflow distinguishes Ready to Pay, Carried to Next Period, and Paid. Ready to Pay 
identifies an amount prepared for disbursement; Carried to Next Period tracks unpaid or unsettled 
amounts transferred forward; Paid denotes confirmed settlement. Payroll and payslip summaries 
present the relevant gross components, deductions, cash advance repayments, carried balances, 
and net amount. A proposed repayment is not a recorded repayment until the appropriate 
payroll/payment event is confirmed and posted. Outstanding cash advance balances are reduced 
only by confirmed repayments.

Cash Advance Management
Employees may submit cash advance requests. Authorized administrators review and approve or 
reject requests; approval is distinct from confirmation that cash was actually given. Once 
disbursement is confirmed, the system tracks the outstanding amount, repayment entries, and 
remaining balance. Payroll summaries distinguish scheduled or projected deductions from 
repayments that have actually been posted.

System Usability Scale (SUS)
The original manuscript reports a SUS score of 80.83, rated Good and Acceptable. This value is 
retained as a reported result, pending reconciliation with all 30 individual questionnaires and the 
item-level calculation sheets. Standard SUS scoring converts responses to 0–4 contributions (odd 
items: response minus 1; even items: 5 minus response), sums contributions and multiplies by 2.5 
for each respondent; the overall SUS score is the mean of respondent scores. Include the 
instrument, individual scoring and summary worksheet in the appendices.

Likert Scale and Computation of Results
The original five-point interpretation intervals are retained: 1.00–1.80 Strongly Disagree; 1.81–
2.60 Disagree; 2.61–3.40 Undecided; 3.41–4.20 Agree; 4.21–5.00 Strongly Agree. The interval 
width is (5 − 1) / 5 = 0.80.
For one item, the frequency-weighted response mean is M = Σ(fᵢ × xᵢ) / N, where fᵢ is the number 
of respondents choosing response value xᵢ, N is the number of valid responses, and Σ denotes 
summation across response categories. For a criterion with k equally weighted items, the average 
of item means is WM = (Σ M ) / k, where M  is the mean for item j. These quantities coincide onlyⱼ ⱼ  
under the applicable weighting and response-completeness assumptions; the final manuscript 
should state which computation was actually used.
