Chapter 4
CONCLUSIONS AND RECOMMENDATIONS
This chapter presents conclusions drawn from the findings of WorkPulse: Employee 
Attendance and Work Analytics Information System for MVL and recommendations for 
further improvement. User-evaluation results are distinguished from technical testing and 
biometric performance evidence.
Conclusions
1. The study identified inaccurate attendance recording, risks of unauthorized or 
proxy attendance, inefficient work-hour monitoring, payroll-processing difficulties, and 
limited employee access to records as major challenges. These findings support the need 
for centralized workforce information management.
2. WorkPulse provides centralized employee records for the Regular, Extra, and 
Manager / Supervisor employee classifications, alongside administrator and employee 
access groups. The documented functions include fingerprint-based attendance, 
authorized manual attendance and corrections with required reasons and correction 
history, employee notifications, leave requests, cash advance management, payroll and 
payslip summaries, descriptive reporting, and analytical features. Cash advance 
workflows encompass requests, approval or rejection, confirmation of disbursement, 
outstanding balances, and repayment tracking. Payroll workflows distinguish Ready to 
Pay, Carried to Next Period, and Paid statuses. Limited offline saving and 
synchronization support designated operations but do not make the entire system offline-
capable.
3. The reported System Usability Scale (SUS) score was 80.83, interpreted in Chapter 
3 as Good and Acceptable. This indicates favorable usability feedback from participating 
respondents; it does not, by itself, establish technical correctness or operational 
reliability.
4. The respondent questionnaire reported weighted means of 4.90 for security, 4.69 
for performance efficiency, and 4.88 for reliability, with an overall weighted mean of

5. Fingerprint attendance depends on the configured scanner, its locally installed 
capture and matching components, and the designated workstation. The study’s 
fingerprint-performance objective must be concluded using actual genuine and 
unrecognized scan trials, timing observations, and the documented computation of 
relevant rates. [INSERT VERIFIED BIOMETRIC RESULTS: trial counts, valid 
comparisons, false acceptances, false rejections, failed captures, processing times, and 
corresponding rates.] No numerical biometric performance conclusion is asserted without

6. WorkPulse has operational limitations. Fresh authentication, server-side approvals, 
fingerprint transactions, and payment-related actions require their applicable online 
services or local workstation components. Offline queues and cached views are limited 
and may contain outdated records; queued actions require synchronization and server 
revalidation. When the fingerprint workstation or device is unavailable, the approved 
fallback is verified administrator manual attendance with a documented reason, 
correction history, and employee notification; a paper attendance record may be used 
until authorized entry is possible.
Recommendations
1. Conduct broader fingerprint-reader and workstation compatibility tests under 
different device conditions. Maintain documented workstation recovery steps, reader 
diagnostics, and a paper-to-authorized-manual-attendance fallback procedure for device 
failure.
2. Improve attendance monitoring beyond the existing daily/date-range filters and 
basic summaries by adding exception-focused views, correction review tools, clearer 
source indicators, and configurable alerts for unusual or unresolved attendance records.
3. Extend payroll and cash advance reporting with reconciliation views, repayment 
histories, carried-balance audit trails, and clearer employee-facing breakdowns. Verify

that settled payroll records cannot be altered by attendance corrections without an 
approved controlled process.
4. Enhance the existing employee notification feature through configurable 
notification preferences, clearer delivery status, and optional additional delivery channels, 
subject to consent and data-protection requirements.
5. Further evaluate and refine existing descriptive and AI-assisted analytical functions 
using sufficient relevant data, documented model versions and performance measures 
where trained models are active, and explicit labels when statistical fallback is used. Do 
not treat forecasts as automatic personnel or payroll decisions.
6. Conduct extended outage, synchronization, and recovery tests, including duplicate 
submissions, stale cached data, local-storage failures, unauthorized access, and 
interrupted connectivity. Record expected and observed outcomes before drawing 
technical reliability conclusions.
7. Complete and report biometric evaluation using actual enrollment and scan-trial 
evidence, including genuine and unrecognized trials, failed captures, measured response 
times, and clearly defined FAR/FRR denominators. Expand testing only with appropriate 
consent and privacy safeguards.
8. Preserve complete supporting materials, including respondent questionnaires, SUS 
scoring sheets, item-level Likert calculations, technical test logs, biometric evaluation 
records, and current deployment screenshots, as appendices or verifiable project 
evidence.
